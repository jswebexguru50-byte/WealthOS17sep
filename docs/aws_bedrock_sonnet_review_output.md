### 1.1 Covariance Matrix Estimation: The Curse of Dimensionality

**The Core Problem**

The current specification implies a standard sample covariance matrix for the Half-Kelly position sizer. For an 805-stock universe, the sample covariance matrix $\hat{\Sigma}$ is a $805 \times 805$ matrix requiring estimation of $\frac{805 \times 806}{2} = 324,415$ unique parameters from typically $T \approx 252$ daily observations. Since $T < N$, the matrix is **singular by construction** — it cannot be inverted, making portfolio optimization mathematically undefined.

**Formal Statement of the Problem**

Let $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times N}$ be the return matrix with $T$ observations and $N$ assets. The sample covariance estimator is:

$$\hat{\Sigma}_{sample} = \frac{1}{T-1} \sum_{t=1}^{T} (\mathbf{r}_t - \bar{\mathbf{r}})(\mathbf{r}_t - \bar{\mathbf{r}})^\top$$

When $T < N$, $\text{rank}(\hat{\Sigma}_{sample}) \leq T - 1 < N$, making $\hat{\Sigma}_{sample}^{-1}$ undefined. Even when $T > N$ (e.g., using 5-year history), the condition number $\kappa(\hat{\Sigma}) = \lambda_{max}/\lambda_{min}$ explodes, causing extreme sensitivity to estimation error — the Markowitz "error maximization" problem.

**The Fix: Ledoit-Wolf Analytical Shrinkage**

The Oracle Approximating Shrinkage (OAS) estimator from Ledoit & Wolf (2004, 2022) provides a closed-form, well-conditioned covariance matrix:

$$\hat{\Sigma}_{LW} = (1 - \alpha^*) \hat{\Sigma}_{sample} + \alpha^* \mu^* \mathbf{I}_N$$

where the optimal shrinkage intensity $\alpha^*$ and shrinkage target scalar $\mu^*$ are computed analytically:

$$\mu^* = \frac{\text{tr}(\hat{\Sigma}_{sample})}{N}$$

$$\rho^2 = \frac{1}{T^2} \sum_{t=1}^{T} \left\| \mathbf{r}_t \mathbf{r}_t^\top - \hat{\Sigma}_{sample} \right\|_F^2$$

$$\gamma^2 = \left\| \hat{\Sigma}_{sample} - \mu^* \mathbf{I}_N \right\|_F^2$$

$$\alpha^* = \min\left(1, \frac{\rho^2}{\gamma^2}\right)$$

For the Indian equity universe with sector clustering, the **Constant Correlation Model** shrinkage target is superior to the identity matrix:

$$\hat{\Sigma}_{CC} = \bar{\rho} \cdot \mathbf{D} \cdot \mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^\top \cdot \mathbf{D} + (1 - \bar{\rho}) \cdot \mathbf{D}^2$$

where $\mathbf{D} = \text{diag}(\hat{\sigma}_1, \ldots, \hat{\sigma}_N)$ and $\bar{\rho} = \frac{2}{N(N-1)} \sum_{i < j} \hat{\rho}_{ij}$ is the average pairwise correlation.

The final shrinkage estimator becomes:

$$\hat{\Sigma}_{LW}^{CC} = (1 - \alpha^*) \hat{\Sigma}_{sample} + \alpha^* \hat{\Sigma}_{CC}$$

**Implementation Invariant (INV-COV-1):**
$$\kappa(\hat{\Sigma}_{LW}) \leq 1000 \quad \text{(condition number bound)}$$
$$\lambda_{min}(\hat{\Sigma}_{LW}) \geq 10^{-6} \quad \text{(positive definiteness guarantee)}$$

### 1.2 Half-Kelly Sizing: Lookahead Bias & Parameter Instability

**Current Flaw: Static Edge Estimation**

The standard Half-Kelly formula $f^* = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{p \cdot b - q}{b}$ uses point estimates of win probability $p$ and payoff ratio $b$ that are typically estimated from the full historical sample — introducing **lookahead bias** and **parameter non-stationarity**.

**Additional Flaw: Ignoring Fat Tails**

The Kelly criterion assumes log-normal returns. Indian equities exhibit excess kurtosis $\kappa \approx 6$–$12$ (versus 3 for normal), meaning the Kelly fraction dramatically over-bets relative to the true optimal under fat-tailed distributions.

**The Fix: Robust Fractional Kelly with Volatility Scaling**

**Step 1: Walk-Forward Edge Estimation**

Estimate $p$ and $b$ using only information available at time $t$ via expanding window:

$$\hat{p}_t = \frac{\sum_{s=1}^{t-1} \mathbf{1}[\text{trade}_s = \text{win}]}{t-1}, \quad \hat{b}_t = \frac{\bar{W}_t}{\bar{L}_t}$$

where $\bar{W}_t$ and $\bar{L}_t$ are the rolling mean win and loss magnitudes computed on out-of-sample trades only.

**Step 2: Fat-Tail Adjusted Kelly**

Under a Student-$t$ distribution with $\nu$ degrees of freedom (estimated via MLE on return series), the Kelly fraction is penalized:

$$f^*_{t} = \frac{\hat{p}_t \cdot \hat{b}_t - (1-\hat{p}_t)}{\hat{b}_t} \cdot \Psi(\nu)$$

where the fat-tail penalty factor is:

$$\Psi(\nu) = \frac{\nu - 2}{\nu} \cdot \frac{1}{1 + \frac{\kappa_e}{6}} \quad \text{for } \nu > 2$$

with $\kappa_e = \frac{6}{\nu - 4}$ being the excess kurtosis of the $t$-distribution (valid for $\nu > 4$).

**Step 3: Volatility-Scaled Fractional Kelly**

$$f^*_{vol-scaled} = \frac{f^*_t}{2} \cdot \frac{\sigma_{target}}{\hat{\sigma}_t^{EWMA}}$$

where:
- $\frac{f^*_t}{2}$ is the half-Kelly fraction (standard institutional practice)
- $\sigma_{target} = 15\%$ annualized (configurable institutional target volatility)
- $\hat{\sigma}_t^{EWMA}$ is the EWMA volatility with decay $\lambda = 0.94$ (RiskMetrics standard):

$$\hat{\sigma}_t^{EWMA} = \sqrt{(1-\lambda) r_{t-1}^2 + \lambda (\hat{\sigma}_{t-1}^{EWMA})^2}$$

**Step 4: Covariance-Adjusted Portfolio Kelly**

For a portfolio of $N$ simultaneous positions, the scalar Kelly must be replaced by the **vector Kelly** that accounts for cross-position correlations:

$$\mathbf{f}^* = \hat{\Sigma}_{LW}^{-1} \cdot \boldsymbol{\mu}_{excess}$$

$$f^*_{portfolio} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{excess}^\top \hat{\Sigma}_{LW}^{-1} \boldsymbol{\mu}_{excess}}{\text{max\_leverage}}$$

where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{excess}$ is the vector of expected excess returns from the Conviction Fusion model.

**Step 5: Transaction Cost Drag Adjustment**

The Kelly fraction must be reduced to account for round-trip transaction costs $c$ (brokerage + STT + exchange fees + impact cost):

For Indian equities, total round-trip cost $c \approx 0.15\%$–$0.35\%$ depending on trade size. The break-even edge condition becomes:

$$\text{Net Edge} = \hat{p}_t \cdot \hat{b}_t - (1-\hat{p}_t) - \frac{c}{|\Delta f|}$$

The position is only initiated if $\text{Net Edge} > 0$ after cost drag. The cost-adjusted Kelly fraction is:

$$f^*_{net} = f^*_{vol-scaled} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{c}{\hat{p}_t \cdot \hat{b}_t - (1-\hat{p}_t)}\right)$$

**Invariant (INV-KELLY-1):** $f^*_{net} \in [0.0, 0.25]$ — hard cap at 25% of portfolio per single position regardless of computed Kelly fraction.

### 1.3 Factor Conviction Fusion: Collinearity & Weighting Flaws

**The Core Problem: Multicollinearity in Factor Space**

The current Conviction Fusion model likely combines factors such as: Price Momentum, Earnings Momentum, Relative Strength, Volume Surge, and Fundamental Quality. These factors are **not orthogonal** — they share substantial common variance. Naively summing weighted factor scores produces a conviction score that double-counts correlated information.

**Formal Collinearity Diagnosis**

Compute the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) for each factor $j$:

$$VIF_j = \frac{1}{1 - R_j^2}$$

where $R_j^2$ is the coefficient of determination from regressing factor $j$ on all other factors. Any factor with $VIF_j > 5$ is collinear and must be orthogonalized or removed.

**The Fix: Principal Component Orthogonalization + Brier-Weighted Fusion**

**Step 1: Factor Standardization**

$$\tilde{f}_{i,j,t} = \frac{f_{i,j,t} - \mu_{j,t}}{\sigma_{j,t}}$$

where $\mu_{j,t}$ and $\sigma_{j,t}$ are cross-sectional mean and standard deviation of factor $j$ at time $t$ (cross-sectional z-scoring).

**Step 2: Orthogonal Factor Extraction via PCA**

Decompose the $N \times K$ standardized factor matrix $\tilde{\mathbf{F}}$:

$$\tilde{\mathbf{F}} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{S} \mathbf{V}^\top$$

Retain the top $K^*$ principal components explaining $\geq 85\%$ of total factor variance:

$$K^* = \min\left\{k : \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k} s_j^2}{\sum_{j=1}^{K} s_j^2} \geq 0.85\right\}$$

The orthogonal factor scores are: $\mathbf{Z} = \tilde{\mathbf{F}} \mathbf{V}_{K^*}$

**Step 3: Brier Score Calibration Weights**

Each original factor $j$ has a historical Brier score $BS_j$ measuring its predictive accuracy:

$$BS_j = \frac{1}{N_{test}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{test}} (p_{i,j} - o_i)^2$$

where $p_{i,j}$ is the predicted probability from factor $j$ and $o_i \in \{0,1\}$ is the realized outcome. Lower Brier score = better calibration.

The Brier-inverse weight for factor $j$:

$$w_j^{Brier} = \frac{(1 - BS_j) \cdot \mathbf{1}[N_j \geq 15]}{\sum_{k=1}^{K} (1 - BS_k) \cdot \mathbf{1}[N_k \geq 15]}$$

**Step 4: Wilson Score Confidence Band Gate**

Before any factor contributes to the fusion score, it must pass the minimum sample gate with a statistically valid confidence interval. The Wilson score interval for win rate $\hat{p}$ with $N$ observations at 95% confidence:

$$CI_{Wilson} = \frac{\hat{p} + \frac{z^2}{2N} \pm z\sqrt{\frac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{N} + \frac{z^2}{4N^2}}}{1 + \frac{z^2}{N}}$$

where $z = 1.96$ for 95% confidence. A factor is **Actionable** only if $CI_{Wilson,lower} > 0.50$ (lower bound of win rate confidence interval exceeds 50%).

**Step 5: Final Orthogonal Conviction Score**

$$\text{OCS}_i = \sum_{j=1}^{K^*} w_j^{Brier} \cdot Z_{i,j} \cdot \mathbf{1}[CI_{Wilson,lower,j} > 0.50]$$

**Invariant (INV-FUSION-1):** If fewer than 3 factors pass the Wilson gate, the signal is classified as **Informational** regardless of OCS magnitude.

#### FAILURE MODE DED-1: Partial Fill Fragmentation Across Temporal Download Windows

**The Problem:**

Consider a Zerodha order for 1,000 shares of RELIANCE executed across three partial fills:

```
Fill A: 09:15:32 — 400 shares @ ₹2,847.50  [Downloaded in Month-1 export]
Fill B: 09:17:44 — 350 shares @ ₹2,848.00  [Downloaded in Month-1 export]
Fill C: 09:22:11 — 250 shares @ ₹2,849.25  [Downloaded in Month-2 export]
```

The current Pass 2 hash `${tradeDate}::${isin}::${tradeType}::${quantity}::${price.toFixed(2)}` treats each fill as a **distinct unique transaction** because quantity and price differ. When Month-1 is re-imported (e.g., during a full historical re-sync), Fills A and B are re-inserted as duplicates because Pass 1 (Trade ID) may not be populated in older Zerodha Console exports, and Pass 2 hashes differ from any existing record.

**The Compounding Problem:** Pass 4 (Day Aggregate Matching) attempts to catch this but uses a `0.01` share tolerance and `5.0%` price tolerance. For a 1,000-share order with fills at ₹2,847.50, ₹2,848.00, and ₹2,849.25, the weighted average is ₹2,848.275. If only Fills A+B exist in DB (750 shares @ ₹2,847.72 avg) and Fill C arrives in a new import, Pass 4 compares:

- DB remaining volume for that day: 750 shares
- Incoming Fill C: 250 shares
- Day aggregate: 1,000 shares total

Pass 4 **cannot distinguish** whether Fill C is a new legitimate partial fill completing the order, or a re-import of an already-recorded fill from a different export format.

#### FAILURE MODE DED-2: Identical Timestamp Collision — Split Orders

**The Problem:**

Algorithmic orders and bracket orders from Zerodha's Kite API frequently generate fills with **identical timestamps** at millisecond resolution when the exchange matching engine processes them in the same tick. Example:

Order 1: BUY 200 INFY @ ₹1,456.00  timestamp: 2024-03-15T10:30:00.000Z
Order 2: BUY 200 INFY @ ₹1,456.00  timestamp: 2024-03-15T10:30:00.000Z

Both orders have **identical Pass 2 hashes** (`2024-03-15::INE009A01021::BUY::200::1456.00`). The current engine will quarantine Order 2 as a duplicate, silently dropping a legitimate 200-share purchase. This creates a **phantom holding deficit** that only surfaces during reconciliation.

  order_id:         string;   // Exchange order ID — groups fills
  broker_seq_no:    number;   // Broker's internal sequence number (monotonic)

// Collision-resistant hash incorporating sequence discriminators
function computeCollisionResistantHash(fill: RawFill): string {
  // Priority 1: Use exchange trade_id if available (globally unique)
  if (fill.trade_id && fill.trade_id.length >= 8) {
    const canonical = `TRADEID::${fill.trade_id.trim().toUpperCase()}::${fill.isin}::${fill.broker_code}`;
    return crypto.createHash('sha256').update(canonical).digest('hex');
  }
  
  // Priority 2: Use exchange sequence number
  if (fill.exchange_seq_no) {
    const canonical = `EXCHSEQ::${fill.exchange_seq_no}::${fill.isin}::${fill.trade_date}::${fill.broker_code}`;
    return crypto.createHash('sha256').update(canonical).digest('hex');

  // Priority 3: Broker sequence number with full fill details

    const canonical = [
      'BROKERSEQ',
      fill.broker_code.toUpperCase(),
      fill.broker_seq_no.toString(),
      fill.isin.toUpperCase(),
      fill.trade_date,
      fill.trade_type.toUpperCase(),
      fill.quantity.toFixed(4),
      fill.price.toFixed(4)
    ].join('::');
    return crypto.createHash('sha256').update(canonical).digest('hex');
  }
  
  // Priority 4: Time-discriminated hash (fallback — collision-prone, flagged for review)
  const canonical = [
    'TIMEDISC',
    fill.trade_date,
    fill.trade_time,           // millisecond precision breaks ties
    fill.isin.toUpperCase(),
    fill.trade_type.toUpperCase(),
    fill.quantity.toFixed(4),
    fill.price.toFixed(4),
    fill.broker_code.toUpperCase()
  ].join('::');
  const hash = crypto.createHash('sha256').update(canonical).digest('hex');
  
  // Flag this record for manual review — no sequence discriminator available
  return `LOWCONF::${hash}`;
}

### 1.1 Bonus Stripping — Section 94(8) ITCA

#### Current Specification Gap

The current specification handles bonus issues purely as a cost-basis conservation event (CA-2). It is **completely silent** on Section 94(8), which creates a disallowance trap that can silently overstate losses and understate tax liability — a forensic audit red flag.

#### Statutory Framework

Section 94(8) of the Income Tax Act, 1961 applies when:

1. A person acquires shares ("original shares") within **3 months before** the record date of a bonus issue
2. Bonus shares are allotted ("bonus shares")
3. The original shares are sold at a **loss** within **9 months after** the record date

**Disallowance Mechanism:**

$$\text{Disallowed Loss} = \min\left(|\text{Loss on Original Shares}|,\ \text{Cost Basis of Bonus Shares Received}\right)$$

The disallowed loss is **not extinguished** — it is **transferred** to reduce the cost basis of the retained bonus shares:

$$\text{Adjusted Cost Basis}_{\text{bonus}} = \text{Original Cost Basis}_{\text{bonus}} - \text{Disallowed Loss}$$

Since bonus shares have zero acquisition cost under Section 55(2)(aa), the adjusted cost basis becomes:

$$\text{Adjusted Cost Basis}_{\text{bonus}} = 0 - \text{Disallowed Loss} = -\text{Disallowed Loss}$$

This negative cost basis is **legally valid** and deferred to the eventual sale of bonus shares.

#### Complete Algorithm — Section 94(8) Bonus Stripping Engine

```
ALGORITHM: BonusStrippingDisallowance_94_8

INPUT:
  original_lot: {buy_date, quantity_original, cost_per_share_original}
  bonus_event: {record_date, bonus_ratio, bonus_shares_allotted}
  sell_event: {sell_date, quantity_sold, sell_price_per_share}

STEP 1: ELIGIBILITY GATE
  days_before_record = record_date - original_lot.buy_date
  days_after_record  = sell_event.sell_date - bonus_event.record_date

  IF days_before_record > 90 DAYS:
    RETURN: Section 94(8) NOT applicable (bought > 3 months before record date)
  IF days_after_record > 270 DAYS:
    RETURN: Section 94(8) NOT applicable (sold > 9 months after record date)
  IF sell_event.sell_price_per_share >= original_lot.cost_per_share_original:
    RETURN: Section 94(8) NOT applicable (no loss on original shares)

STEP 2: COMPUTE GROSS LOSS ON ORIGINAL SHARES
  gross_loss = (original_lot.cost_per_share_original
                - sell_event.sell_price_per_share)
               × sell_event.quantity_sold

STEP 3: COMPUTE COST BASIS OF BONUS SHARES
  # Bonus shares have zero cost under Section 55(2)(aa)
  cost_basis_bonus = 0.00

STEP 4: COMPUTE DISALLOWANCE
  disallowed_loss = min(gross_loss, cost_basis_bonus)
  # Since cost_basis_bonus = 0, disallowed_loss = 0 in standard case.
  # HOWEVER: If original shares had been acquired via a prior corporate
  # action (e.g., demerger) with a non-zero allocated cost, then
  # cost_basis_bonus may be non-zero. See Section 1.3 for demerger cost.
  
  # For shares with allocated cost from demerger:
  IF bonus_shares_have_allocated_cost:
    disallowed_loss = min(gross_loss, allocated_cost_of_bonus_shares)

STEP 5: ADJUST REPORTED LOSS
  reported_loss_on_original = gross_loss - disallowed_loss

STEP 6: TRANSFER DISALLOWANCE TO BONUS LOT COST BASIS
  FOR EACH bonus_lot IN inventory WHERE source = bonus_event.id:
    bonus_lot.adjusted_cost_per_share -= (disallowed_loss / bonus_lot.quantity)
    bonus_lot.section_94_8_adjustment = disallowed_loss
    bonus_lot.adjustment_audit_trail = {
      original_sell_date: sell_event.sell_date,
      original_loss_disallowed: disallowed_loss,
      statutory_reference: "Section 94(8) ITCA"
    }

OUTPUT:
  reported_loss: gross_loss - disallowed_loss
  bonus_lot_adjusted_cost: -disallowed_loss (per share basis)
```

### 1.2 Dividend Stripping — Section 94(7) ITCA

#### Statutory Framework

Section 94(7) applies when:

1. Shares/units acquired within **3 months before** the record date of dividend
2. Dividend is received (now taxable as "Income from Other Sources" post-Finance Act 2020)
3. Shares sold at a **loss** within **3 months after** the record date

**Disallowance Formula:**

$$\text{Disallowed Loss} = \min\left(|\text{Capital Loss on Shares}|,\ \text{Dividend Received}\right)$$

The disallowed loss reduces the capital loss that can be set off under Section 70/74.

#### Algorithm — Section 94(7) Dividend Stripping Engine

```
ALGORITHM: DividendStrippingDisallowance_94_7

INPUT:
  lot: {buy_date, quantity, cost_per_share}
  dividend_event: {record_date, dividend_per_share, total_dividend_received}
  sell_event: {sell_date, quantity_sold, sell_price_per_share}

STEP 1: ELIGIBILITY GATE
  days_before_record = record_date - lot.buy_date
  days_after_record  = sell_event.sell_date - dividend_event.record_date

  IF days_before_record > 90 DAYS: RETURN not_applicable
  IF days_after_record  > 90 DAYS: RETURN not_applicable
  IF sell_price_per_share >= cost_per_share: RETURN not_applicable (no loss)

STEP 2: COMPUTE CAPITAL LOSS
  capital_loss = (cost_per_share - sell_price_per_share) × quantity_sold

STEP 3: COMPUTE DISALLOWANCE
  disallowed_loss = min(capital_loss, dividend_event.total_dividend_received)

STEP 4: ADJUST REPORTED CAPITAL LOSS
  reportable_capital_loss = capital_loss - disallowed_loss

OUTPUT:
  reportable_capital_loss: capital_loss - disallowed_loss
  disallowed_amount: disallowed_loss
  statutory_reference: "Section 94(7) ITCA"
  
NOTE: The disallowed amount is NOT transferred anywhere — it is permanently
      extinguished. This differs from Section 94(8) where it transfers to
      the bonus lot cost basis.
```

### 1.3 Demerger Cost Apportionment — Sections 49(2C) and 49(2D)

#### Current Specification Gap

The specification mentions demergers in CA-3 but uses a simplified formula. The actual statutory mechanism under Sections 49(2C) and 49(2D) is **significantly more complex** and depends on whether the demerger is a **"tax-neutral demerger"** under Section 2(19AA).

#### Statutory Framework

**Section 49(2C):** Cost of shares in the **resulting company** received by shareholders:

$$C_{\text{resulting}} = C_{\text{demerged}} \times \frac{\text{NBV of Assets Transferred to Resulting Co.}}{\text{NBV of Total Assets of Demerged Co.}}$$

**Section 49(2D):** Adjusted cost of **retained shares** in the demerged company:

$$C_{\text{demerged, adjusted}} = C_{\text{demerged}} - C_{\text{resulting}}$$

Where **NBV = Net Book Value** as per the books of the demerged company **immediately before** the demerger.

#### Critical Distinction: NBV vs FMV

The specification must be explicit: **Indian tax law uses NBV, NOT FMV** for this apportionment. This is a common error in financial software.

$$\text{Apportionment Ratio} = \frac{\text{NBV}_{\text{transferred assets}}}{\text{NBV}_{\text{total assets of demerged company}}}$$

**NOT:**

$$\text{WRONG: Apportionment Ratio} \neq \frac{\text{FMV}_{\text{resulting company}}}{\text{FMV}_{\text{demerged company}} + \text{FMV}_{\text{resulting company}}}$$

The FMV-based ratio (market price ratio on ex-date) is commonly used in practice but is **not the statutory formula**. The specification must flag this discrepancy and provide both calculations with an audit warning.

#### Complete Demerger Cost Apportionment Algorithm

```
ALGORITHM: DemergerCostApportionment_49_2C_2D

INPUT:
  original_lot: {
    symbol_demerged: "PARENTCO",
    buy_date: date,
    quantity: Q,
    cost_per_share: C,
    total_cost: Q × C
  }
  demerger_event: {
    record_date: date,
    resulting_company: "SPINOFFCO",
    shares_received_ratio: R,  -- e.g., 1 share of SPINOFFCO per 2 shares of PARENTCO
    nbv_transferred: NBV_T,    -- Net Book Value of assets transferred (from scheme)
    nbv_total: NBV_D,          -- Total Net Book Value of demerged company
    fmv_demerged_ex_date: FMV_D,   -- Market price of PARENTCO on ex-date (for cross-check)
    fmv_resulting_ex_date: FMV_R   -- Market price of SPINOFFCO on ex-date (for cross-check)
  }

STEP 1: COMPUTE STATUTORY APPORTIONMENT RATIO (NBV-BASED)
  ratio_statutory = NBV_T / NBV_D
  
  ASSERT: 0 < ratio_statutory < 1
  ASSERT: NBV_T and NBV_D sourced from NCLT-approved demerger scheme document
  
STEP 2: COMPUTE MARKET-BASED RATIO (FOR AUDIT CROSS-CHECK ONLY)
  total_fmv = FMV_D + (FMV_R × R)  -- Adjusted for share exchange ratio
  ratio_market = (FMV_R × R) / total_fmv
  
  # Flag if deviation > 20% between statutory and market ratio
  IF |ratio_statutory - ratio_market| > 0.20:
    EMIT WARNING: "Significant divergence between NBV ratio ({ratio_statutory:.4f}) 
                   and market ratio ({ratio_market:.4f}). 
                   Verify NBV figures from NCLT scheme document.
                   Statutory NBV ratio governs for tax purposes."

STEP 3: COMPUTE COST ALLOCATED TO RESULTING COMPANY SHARES
  cost_allocated_to_resulting = original_lot.total_cost × ratio_statutory
  shares_received_in_resulting = original_lot.quantity × R  -- Apply exchange ratio
  cost_per_share_resulting = cost_allocated_to_resulting / shares_received_in_resulting

STEP 4: COMPUTE ADJUSTED COST OF RETAINED DEMERGED SHARES
  cost_retained_demerged = original_lot.total_cost × (1 - ratio_statutory)
  cost_per_share_demerged_adjusted = cost_retained_demerged / original_lot.quantity

STEP 5: HOLDING PERIOD CONTINUITY
  # Per Section 49(2C) proviso: Holding period of resulting company shares
  # includes the holding period of original demerged company shares
  resulting_lot.buy_date = original_lot.buy_date  -- INHERITED, not demerger record date
  
  # This is CRITICAL for Section 112A grandfathering:
  # If original shares were held before Jan 31, 2018, the resulting company
  # shares also qualify for grandfathering with inherited holding period.

STEP 6: GRANDFATHERING ADJUSTMENT FOR RESULTING COMPANY SHARES
  IF original_lot.buy_date < 2018-01-31:
    # The resulting company shares inherit grandfathering eligibility
    # FMV of resulting company shares as of Jan 31, 2018 must be determined
    # This requires the FMV of SPINOFFCO on Jan 31, 2018 (if it existed)
    # OR a proportional allocation of PARENTCO's Jan 31, 2018 FMV
    
    IF resulting_company_listed_before_2018_01_31:
      fmv_resulting_jan2018 = market_price(SPINOFFCO, 2018-01-31)
    ELSE:
      # Resulting company listed after Jan 31, 2018 due to demerger
      # Use proportional allocation of PARENTCO's Jan 31, 2018 FMV
      fmv_parentco_jan2018 = market_price(PARENTCO, 2018-01-31)
      fmv_resulting_jan2018 = fmv_parentco_jan2018 × ratio_statutory / R
      # Flag: This is a grey area; CBDT has not issued explicit guidance.
      # Conservative approach: use cost_per_share_resulting as floor.
      fmv_resulting_jan2018 = max(fmv_resulting_jan2018, cost_per_share_resulting)

OUTPUT:
  resulting_lot: {
    symbol: SPINOFFCO,
    quantity: shares_received_in_resulting,
    cost_per_share: cost_per_share_resulting,
    buy_date: original_lot.buy_date,  -- INHERITED
    grandfathered_fmv_jan2018: fmv_resulting_jan2018,
    source: "DEMERGER_49_2C",
    parent_lot_id: original_lot.id,
    nbv_ratio_used: ratio_statutory

## PART I: DECISION ERGONOMICS & OCULAR FATIGUE ANALYSIS

### 1.1 The 5-Second Alpha Evaluation Framework

The human visual system processes financial information through three sequential cognitive stages. Each stage has a measurable time budget that must be respected by the interface architecture:

```
Stage 1: Pre-attentive Processing    (0ms – 250ms)
         ↓ Color, size, motion, orientation
         ↓ "Is this opportunity positive or negative?"

Stage 2: Attentive Scanning          (250ms – 2,000ms)
         ↓ Pattern recognition, spatial grouping
         ↓ "What is the magnitude and conviction level?"

Stage 3: Deliberate Evaluation       (2,000ms – 5,000ms)
         ↓ Semantic reading, numerical comparison
         ↓ "Do I act? What size? What risk?"
```

**Critical Failure Modes in the Current Specification:**

The existing Section 3 specification contains several ergonomic deficiencies that violate the 5-second evaluation constraint:

1. **Semantic Overload:** The current palette defines 18 light-mode tokens and 12 dark-mode tokens. Research on pre-attentive processing (Treisman & Gelade, 1980; Healey & Enns, 2012) establishes that humans can reliably distinguish a maximum of **5–7 simultaneous color categories** without cognitive penalty. The current 18-token system creates semantic ambiguity between `--fin-info` (#1D5FA8) and `--brand-pri` (#0B3D91) — both are blue, both appear in data contexts, and they are perceptually indistinguishable at small sizes.

2. **Missing Visual Hierarchy Enforcement:** The specification defines typographic scales but provides no **spatial weight hierarchy** — no formal rule governing which data element commands the eye first, second, and third within a card. Without this, the eye wanders, consuming the full 5-second budget on orientation rather than evaluation.

3. **Contrast Ratio Gaps (Quantified Below):** Several existing tokens fail WCAG 2.1 AAA at the sizes specified. `--text-sec` (#4B5568 on #FFFFFF) achieves only **5.74:1** — passing AA but failing AAA. For 12px uppercase table headers (the most common usage), WCAG 2.1 requires **4.5:1 minimum for large text** but the specification mandates AAA (7:1). This is a compliance gap.

4. **Dark Mode Glassmorphism Penalty:** `rgba(15, 23, 42, 0.75)` as a card background creates a **variable effective contrast ratio** depending on what content renders behind the blur. This is architecturally incompatible with a guaranteed 7:1 contrast standard. The specification must define a **minimum opaque equivalent** for contrast calculation purposes.

### 1.2 Cognitive Load Reduction Principles

The following principles must be encoded as inviolable design invariants:

**Principle 1 — The Foveal Anchor Rule:**
Every card must have exactly one element that captures foveal attention within 150ms. For the Alpha Dossier, this is the **Conviction Score** rendered at ≥32px with maximum contrast. All other elements are subordinate.

**Principle 3 — Semantic Color Exclusivity:**
Each semantic color must map to **exactly one financial meaning**. Blue cannot mean both "brand action" and "cash/info." The palette must be refactored to enforce this exclusivity.

Below 10 data points, the card feels sparse and requires navigation to find critical data. Above 18 data points, the card requires deliberate reading rather than scanning.
