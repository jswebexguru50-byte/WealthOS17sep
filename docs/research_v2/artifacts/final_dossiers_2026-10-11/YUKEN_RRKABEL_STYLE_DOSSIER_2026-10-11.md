## Executive assessment

Yuken India is an Indian hydraulics manufacturer serving machine-tool, infrastructure and construction applications through manufacturing locations in Karnataka, Maharashtra and Haryana. Its FY25 consolidated income was ₹461.09 crore, up 7.83% year on year, while consolidated PAT rose to ₹24.60 crore from ₹18.79 crore. Audited operating cash flow was ₹49.34 crore, but reported investment in PPE/intangibles including advances was ₹54.74 crore; interpretation of the resulting funding/return profile needs a fuller multi-year capex and balance-sheet extraction. [S1]

The available primary evidence supports a detailed operating, cash-flow, foreign-exchange and related-party review. It does not yet establish order-book visibility, customer concentration, supplier concentration, market share, capacity utilisation or debt maturity structure. The executive conclusion is therefore evidence-limited: documented business capability and positive FY25 earnings/cash generation coexist with demand cyclicality, overseas-currency exposure and incomplete concentration/capital-allocation data.

### Q2. Business model, offering and operating footprint

**Evidence state:** Partially evidenced — company-stated differentiators and demand constraints are available; peer comparison, market share and independent durability testing are pending. [S1]

## Evidence, interpretation and completion record

This section expands the question-level evidence states above. It is included to make the dossier auditable: a reader can distinguish a company disclosure, an independently recomputed XBRL value, a provider snapshot, and a missing input. No missing input is filled by a proxy unless expressly identified.

### Business model and industry evidence (Q2–Q6)

**What is evidenced.** The annual report gives a coherent manufacturing value chain: product engineering and system design feed hydraulic power units, valves, solenoids, cylinders, motors, pumps, castings and precision-machined parts. It explicitly ties the offering to Indian industrial needs, machine tools, infrastructure and construction. The cited sites in Karnataka, Maharashtra and Haryana are production locations, while the offices/dealers/service-partner language supports a domestic go-to-market footprint. [S1] This permits a factual business description, but not a statement of segment economics, capacity leadership or market share.

**What is not evidenced.** The annual-report statements reviewed do not name customers or quantify customer/channel mix. They do not state installed capacity, capacity utilisation, order-book size, backlog conversion, lead times or contractual renewals. Because these omissions materially affect the interpretation of cyclical revenue, no inferred order book, captive customer relationship or utilisation conclusion is appropriate. The final report should retrieve: (i) the annual-report credit-risk/customer-concentration note; (ii) results presentations and official quarterly transcripts; (iii) exchange announcements of material orders; and (iv) any lender/rating report that supplies capacity or customer detail. A valid result may still be `not disclosed`.

**Competitive-position test.** The company’s description of customised solutions, local engineering and a broad service/dealer footprint supports a *company-stated* differentiation narrative. It does not independently prove pricing power or switching costs. To close Q3, the process needs a peer set appropriate to hydraulic components, period-matched operating margins/returns, patent/certification evidence, customer-retention evidence and a quantified split between proprietary/system content and commoditised components. Until then, the demand slowdown the company identifies is a more reliable risk signal than a moat conclusion. [S1]

**Input and market test.** The currency note is meaningful primary evidence: it identifies both overseas supplier transactions and monetary financial assets/liabilities in several currencies. The disclosed receivable/payable currency amounts show exposure exists but do not establish hedging policy effectiveness, raw-material pass-through, or a single-source supplier risk. The next extraction needs raw-material, import, inventory and purchase disclosures, followed by dated commodity/currency movements only if the company itself identifies those as material. [S1]

### Financial quality and allocation evidence (Q7–Q17)

**Period discipline.** Yuken is a priority case for the 29Q period guardrail. NSE XBRL uses `OneD` for a true discrete quarter and `FourD` for year-to-date/full-year contexts. The controlled benchmark gives FY25 consolidated discrete revenue of ₹110.20 crore, ₹115.6571 crore, ₹106.8465 crore and ₹124.651 crore; treating the Q2 YTD ₹225.8588 crore as Q2 revenue would materially overstate quarter performance. The same discipline applies to PAT. [S2] Any final quarterly narrative must cite a reconciled discrete dataset, not spreadsheet values whose context is unknown.

**Earnings.** Consolidated income increased by ₹33.47 crore in FY25, while PAT increased by ₹5.82 crore. [S1] The report’s PBITDA line supports a reported pre-interest/depreciation profitability observation, but the dossier does not label it EBITDA because definitions and adjustments need checking. The nil exceptional-item entry is helpful, but it is not enough to call all profit recurring: other income, impairment, associate contribution, tax and non-cash items must still be reviewed in the notes. That is the required bridge from reported PAT to analytical normalised PAT.

**Cash and reinvestment.** FY25 consolidated CFO of ₹49.34 crore exceeded FY25 PAT; this is an encouraging one-year conversion datapoint. Yet capex of ₹54.74 crore exceeded CFO before financing items, and cash-flow financing includes a mix of long-term repayment, long-term proceeds and net short-term borrowing. [S1] That combination does not prove value-destructive investment; it says the research must establish whether capex was maintenance, capacity expansion, new products, replacement or advances. The key next documents are PPE/intangible additions by asset class, capex commitments, project commentary, and FY24–FY26 discrete cash-flow statements. Only then may research calculate reinvestment rate, incremental return or sustainable free cash flow.

**Provider snapshot reconciliation.** The Trendlyne metrics (ROCE 7.76%, ROE 3.87%, debt/equity 0.28 and P/E 98.63x) are retained because they are useful coverage inputs, but are not promoted to conclusions. [T1] Before use, the pipeline must persist the provider’s `as_of`, reporting scope, unit, numerator/denominator definition and price date, then compare it against validated primary facts. This is particularly important for P/E where price date and EPS scope can create a misleading multiple.

**Valuation and scenario discipline.** The record currently cannot support own-history percentile, direct-peer comparison, FCF yield or reverse DCF. A proper valuation workpaper must include price as-of date, diluted share count, net debt/cash/lease treatment, explicit forecast periods, tax rate and terminal assumptions. A scenario table must identify a disclosed fact, an analytical assumption and a counter-fact for each case. The absence of that workpaper is why this dossier provides no valuation call.

### Governance, risk and balance-sheet evidence (Q18–Q27)

**Governance evidence.** The annual report identifies senior management, statutory auditor, board-meeting count, independent-director declarations and Risk Management Committee activity. [S1] Those disclosures evidence governance processes; they do not establish governance quality in the absence of a tenure, fee, attendance, board-skill and regulatory-history review. The annual report’s historical late-RPT-disclosure fine should be tracked as a dated compliance fact. It is neither omitted nor treated as a current enforcement finding without fresh evidence.

**Related parties.** The report’s RPT note gives named related entities and transaction values, which is more actionable than generic governance language. [S1] The final assessment needs a full counterparty table covering purchases, sales/services, loans/guarantees, year-end receivables/payables, terms and previous-year change. Materiality should be computed against the relevant revenue/assets/net-worth denominator after each number is scope-aligned. An arm’s-length statement is management disclosure, not a substitute for that quantitative test.

**Risk and solvency test.** The financial-risk note provides direct evidence of currency risk and the annual report acknowledges cyclical customer-sector conditions. [S1] The dossier does not calculate interest-rate sensitivity because no floating-rate borrowing split is available. It does not calculate coverage, net debt/EBITDA or maturity concentration because gross debt, cash, lease liabilities, maturities and covenant terms need extraction. A default/no-default statement or debt/equity provider ratio cannot safely substitute for them.

**Ownership and watchlist.** The provider ownership snapshot must be verified against an official quarterly shareholding pattern. This is mandatory before a pledge or ownership-change conclusion. The operational watchlist is deliberately a variable list rather than a set of invented alerts: repeatable baseline thresholds require at least eight validated quarters and matching ownership periods. The data model should retain both raw evidence and status (`available`, `missing`, `conflict`, `not applicable`) so the app cannot silently turn a missing fact into a clean score.

## Source-use protocol and analytical boundaries

The annual report governs FY25 audited financial statements, management discussion, risk disclosures, governance and related-party notes. [S1] The XBRL reconciliation benchmark controls period classification for the figures it covers and exists to prevent a cumulative `FourD` fact being shown as a quarterly result. [S2] The provider snapshot is supplementary, used for discovery and cross-checking; it cannot override an audited primary document. [T1]

Figures retain their stated scope. ₹ lakh annual-report figures are converted to crore only by dividing by 100; an XBRL/provider number is not converted until its unit, scale and context are checked. Any numeric claim without period, scope, unit and evidence ID is ineligible for final publication.

The material unresolved questions are customer concentration, capex nature/return, the most-current discrete-quarter direction, working-capital behaviour, debt/cash/lease maturity and related-party balances/terms. Their absence is not a negative finding. It means the available evidence cannot support a complete quality, leverage or valuation conclusion. No media allegation, third-party forecast or market multiple is promoted without traceable primary evidence. This dossier gives no price target, rating or transaction recommendation.

## Question coverage audit

Q1, Q28 and Q29 are deliberately deferred by the approved fundamental-only scope. Q2, Q5, Q7, Q10, Q12–Q16, Q18–Q23 and Q27 contain at least one primary or controlled-system fact. Q3, Q4, Q6, Q8, Q9, Q11, Q17 and Q24–Q26 remain partial because the essential subquestion inputs are incomplete. This distinction matters: an answer that says `unknown` after an identified source route has been checked is more reliable than an answer that converts an industry generalisation into a company fact.

For Q4, the next search is customer concentration in credit-risk notes, not an internet search for speculative customer names. For Q5, supplier/foreign-currency notes should be read alongside raw-material purchases. For Q6, the final report needs a dated, attributable market-size source and a company-specific penetration measure. For Q8, returns must be calculated using scope-consistent NOPAT and invested capital. For Q11, segment tables must be taken from notes rather than a product brochure. For Q17, every valuation multiple needs a common price date and denominator. For Q24–Q26, a debt schedule and lease breakdown are indispensable.

The permanent evidence store should keep search attempts as well as successful facts. That makes it possible to show why a field remains unavailable, prevents repeated low-value retrieval, and gives the next run an exact source/action path. It also preserves the distinction between an unavailable company disclosure and a document that has not yet been parsed.
